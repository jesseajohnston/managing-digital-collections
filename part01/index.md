## Exploring the Intersections of Open Culture and Cultural Heritage

This book starts from the premise that "open source" reflects desirable information values
that can be supportive of ethical access, empowered and informed technology use, and provide the most appropriate technology model for libraries, archives, and museums.
That is not to say all software is free, that we should use subpar tools, or that every cultural heritage worker needs to be an information technologist. It does, however, suggest that open source is the best framework for making digital content available, from software platforms to metadata, in digital cultural heritage management.

The book aims to elaborate what "open source" can mean, and what it has meant, in libraries and archives.
To do that, the book begins by exploring the scope of "open source," its relationships to various ideas and understandings in libraries and archives, and a variety of ways that can play out,
in both software development and in digital cultural heritage.
Throughout, the goal is to demonstrate these values and possibilities with examples and values from projects
undertaken by libraries, archives, or museums (LAMs) or similar collecting institutions.

This section begins with an exploration of various understandings of "open source," since there are varied understandings and discussions of this term in both software and systems development.
At the same time, different trajectories of openness have developed in libraries and archvies, including pursuits of open access publishing and a long tradition of exchanging bibliographic data.
The second part of this section explores the history and intersection of openness between technology and library/archives technology domains.
As illustrations of open source architecture and the potentials of networking, the
third section discusses open technologies as the basis of the world wide web.
Finally, the section closes with a discussion of the growing conversation on open metadata.
