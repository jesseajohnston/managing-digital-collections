## Abstract

This book offers a practical, project-oriented primer
for people interested in learning about management of digital content in cultural heritage collections.
Thus, the text is written with the assumption that readers and users are working in, or interested in libraries, archives, museums, or other organizations that focus on collections.
In addition, the text assumes a functional level of knowledge about using
programmatic digital tools, including the Python programming language,
text-based command line interface, and other system-level tools that
facilitate the bulk and batch management of digital content in small-
and medium-sized collections.

## Acknowledgements

While the form and content of this book were assembled largely by the primary author, no project like this takes shape on its own.
Many people helped the author to shape and improve this book.
One of the primary shapers of the course that served as the basis for this book, though he was not directly involved in creating the current resource, was Carl Lagoze. Carl initially designed and taught a version of "Networked Services" in 2020,
and I inherited his syllabus in 2022. That original design provided the conceptual underpinning and structure, though I made significant changes in tools and updated methods in the ensuing years. Any errors or shortcomings are likely the product of my changes and modifications.

Additionally, the groups of students at the University of Michigan School of Information in 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025 actually used the content, tested the assignments and examples,
and offered insightful feedback that improved the current resource.

Finally, thank you to the community and culture of the University of Michigan School of Information (UMSI).
UMSI provided significant resources and infrastructure in
creating and supporting the various versions of the original course.
The interest and research groups of faculty and students also provided significant inspiration.
I do not think a similarly technically sophisticated and deep course could have been successful in another iSchool.
I hope, therefore, that this book carries forward UMSI's dedication
to training the next generation of the most critical, engaged, and digitally-savvy archivists, librarians, and related information professionals.
